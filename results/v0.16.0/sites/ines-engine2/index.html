What I shoot

Six kinds of work, one way of seeing.

Each one is lit by whatever light the room already has, directed only as far as it needs to be, and edited to look like the day rather than a brochure of it.

Weddings, in order

Full-day coverage from the first cup of tea to the last dance. Ceremonies are shot from the side aisle so no one is turned around for the photographs.

Editorial portraits

Ninety minutes on location with one lens. You choose the window or the doorway, and I build the picture around how you stand in it.

Restaurants and kitchens

Hands, heat and service at full speed, photographed without stopping the kitchen. Menu images delivered within 48 hours of service.

Hotels and rooms

Rooms at first light and blue hour, when the lamps are on and the linen is still made. Full art-direction on set if the shoot needs it.

Shot on film

Portra 400 and Ektar 100 in 35mm and medium format, roughly one roll in three of every session. Scanned at 3200 dpi by hand, never by an automatic preset.

Albums and archive

Hand-bound albums in linen and paper, archival prints, and a private gallery kept online for ten years so you can order again at any age.

Process

How a shoot works, in three steps.

  1. 01

    A twenty-minute call, then a walk

    We talk about the day, the people and what you want to feel when you look back. Then we walk the location together and I note where the light falls at the hour you have booked.

  2. 02

    Shoot in the light you have

    No more than two set-ups an hour. I ask for small movements, not poses, and I keep a film camera in hand for the moments you will not notice me taking.

  3. 03

    Edit, grade, deliver

    Every frame is culled and graded by hand in my Lisbon studio. You receive a sneak peek of ten pictures within 48 hours and the full gallery in about four weeks.

About

Eleven years behind the camera, and still learning the light.

I started with my grandmother's Nikon FM2 in a flat in Mouraria and have shot on film ever since. I assisted two Lisbon documentary photographers for four years before taking my own bookings in 2015.

I work alone on most jobs, sometimes with one assistant, and I would rather miss a shot than interrupt a moment to take it.
