Our rivers

Six ways we work on the water

Each one is aimed at a specific problem on chalk streams: silted gravel, eroding banks, invasive plants and a shortage of data about what is living in the river.

Late summer and autumn

Gravel riffle restoration

We place graded spawning gravel where decades of silt have buried the riffles, so wild brown trout can spawn again.

Planting days, Nov to March

Volunteer planting days

Crews plant sedge, water mint, alder and willow on bare banks each winter. Tools, gloves and tea are provided on site.

Pulled before seed set

Invasive species control

We pull Himalayan balsam before it seeds in August and treat Japanese knotweed on monitored plots, checking each site twice a year.

Monthly, year-round

Citizen-science monitoring

Trained volunteers sample invertebrates and phosphate at 92 sites and upload every result to a public river record.

Coir rolls and willow spiling

Bank stabilisation

Coir rolls and living willow hold eroding banks while roots establish, so we avoid concrete and keep the bank line natural.

Whole school year

River lessons for schools

Year 5 and 6 classes study the same stretch of water across the year, with sampling kits and a field day on site.

How your gift works

From your pound to the water, in three steps

  1. 1

    You choose a stretch of water

    Pick a riffle, a bank or a monitoring site from our map. We confirm the cost before any work is booked, so you know exactly what your gift will buy.

  2. 2

    Crews do the work within 60 days

    Contractors and volunteers place gravel, plant or clear the site, and we record the work. The 13p in every pound we do not spend on rivers covers insurance, training and administration.

  3. 3

    You see what changed

    Every site has a public page with before-and-after photographs, invertebrate counts and fish surveys, updated after each visit.
