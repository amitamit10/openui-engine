Bring the chalk streams back, one riffle at a time.

Since 2014 we have re-laid 38 km of gravel, replanted kilometres of bank and pulled invasive weed from the Sallow, Coldharbour and Hallow Bottom reaches. Every gift and every spade is logged, so you can see exactly what changed.

Our rivers

Five kinds of work that bring a chalk stream back to life

Chalk streams are rare: only around 200 exist worldwide, and most of England's are in the south. Our work targets the things that actually limit them, from silted gravel to bankside shade to water that nobody has measured.

  • Gravel and riffle restoration

    We re-lay clean flint gravel where fine silt has smothered the bed, so trout and mayfly larvae have somewhere to spawn and hide. Each riffle is pegged, measured and resurveyed.

  • Volunteer planting days

    Every second Saturday, crews plant 1,500 native sedges, water mint and brooklime by hand. Kit, tea and a short induction are provided. Groups of 6 or more can book a dedicated day.

  • Invasive species removal

    Himalayan balsam and Japanese knotweed are pulled before they seed, then bagged and composted off site. A single missed plant can reseed a whole reach, so we revisit each stretch three times a year.

  • Water-quality monitoring

    Trained citizen scientists test nitrate, phosphate, pH and dissolved oxygen at 57 fixed sites every month, using the same kits since 2016. The readings go straight into the public river log.

  • School and corporate days

    Year 5 classes from Marlow Lane Primary and staff teams from our partner firms come out to survey, tag and plant. Each visit comes with a site map and a short activity pack for the classroom.

  • Open river maps

    Every site, planting day and monitoring result is on one public map of the three catchments. Anglers, landowners and parish councils can check progress on their own stretch of water.

How your gift works

From your donation to gravel in the water in three steps

  1. 1

    Your gift buys specific work

    A £40 gift pays for a 10 m riffle: 6 tonnes of washed flint, the survey pegs and two days of crew time. Donations are allocated to a named site before the work is planned.

  2. 2

    Crews and volunteers do the work

    Our three paid river officers lead every job, with volunteers doing the planting and clearance. Landowners sign a 10-year agreement, so the work is protected once it is finished.

  3. 3

    You see the result, measured

    Each site gets a before-and-after survey and a line in our quarterly river report, including the things that did not work. You can follow the site your gift paid for on the open map.
