What you get

Everything a class needs, and nothing a lecture hall would.

Six learners, one teacher and a weekly hour in which the topic is chosen by the group. Here is what sits around that table.

  • Six seats, no hiding

    Every live class is capped at six learners and one teacher. Cameras are optional, and by lesson two the teacher will know how you pronounce your own surname.

  • Tuesday Table speaking club

    One hour a week on a topic the group picks: a flat viewing that went wrong, a train strike, a suspicious haircut. Drop in from any level, including the one you think is too early for you.

  • A progress report a human wrote

    Every four weeks your teacher writes you a single page: what you can now say, the three errors that keep coming back, and one thing to try before the next class. No template, no algorithm.

  • Built for moving, not just studying

    Modules on landlords, GP appointments, school forms and small talk at the bins, in the order you will actually need them. Week one is not about the subjunctive.

  • Say it, hear it, fix it

    Record a two-minute answer each week in the pronunciation lab and get timestamped notes on vowel length, Japanese pitch accent and the Polish consonant clusters that keep ambushing you.

  • Teachers who were the new person

    Ana moved from Porto to Leeds at 29 with two suitcases and no kettle. Krzysztof left Gdańsk for Osaka and still cannot get a lease without a fight. They teach from experience, not from a textbook sketch of Lisbon.

How learning works

Three steps from a nervous hello to a class you look forward to.

  1. 1

    Book a 45-minute trial

    You talk with a teacher about your goals, your diary and your nerves. We place you at the right level, not the most flattering one.

  2. 2

    Join a class of six

    Classes start on the first Monday of each term. Each group meets twice a week at the same time with the same six faces, so nobody has to introduce themselves twice.

  3. 3

    Speak on Tuesday, read on the fourth Friday

    Come to Tuesday Table to practise out loud. Four weeks in, your teacher sends a progress report that is honest, specific and occasionally funny.
