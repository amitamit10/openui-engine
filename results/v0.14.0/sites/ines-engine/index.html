01 What I photograph

Six kinds of work, one way of looking.

Whether it is a wedding in a Sintra garden or a dining room at four in the afternoon, I work with the light that is already there and keep the room calm enough for people to forget the camera.

  • Weddings

    Full days or half days, from the walk to the church to the last dance. The ceremony is shot from the side so the room stays in the frame. A second photographer on request.

  • Editorial portraits

    One sitter, one window and about forty minutes. I shoot film for the slow portraits and digital for the rest, and I brief the sitter as little as possible.

  • Restaurant campaigns

    Dishes, hands and the room at the hour the light is right. Images are graded for menus, websites and printed walls, and I bring my own reflector and no stands.

  • Hotel and boutique rooms

    Rooms photographed empty and lived in, with the bed made the way your housekeeping team actually makes it. Four to eight rooms in a day, with notes for the next visit.

  • Film and digital

    35mm and 6×6 on Portra 400 and Tri-X, scanned at 6000 px. The digital set is delivered alongside, so you can compare the two and choose.

  • Printed albums

    Linen-bound books of forty to eighty pages, designed with you and proofed on paper before a single copy is printed. Delivered in a slipcase.

02 Process

How a shoot works, in three steps.

  1. 01

    Call and dates

    A thirty-minute call, then a written quote within one working day. Your date is held with a 20% deposit, and nothing else is due until the scouting visit.

  2. 02

    Scout the light

    For weddings I visit the venue at the same hour as your ceremony. For portraits and commercial work we choose the window together, usually on a weekday morning when the restaurant is quiet.

  3. 03

    Shoot, then deliver

    I shoot in natural light only. You receive a private gallery, edited and graded, in an average of 19 days, with full-resolution files and the film scans alongside.
