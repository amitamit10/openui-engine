Our rivers

Six kinds of work, one goal: a river that runs clean and fast over gravel.

Most of our days are spent in waders on the bank. Here is what happens out there, and why each job matters to the fish, the insects and the people who use the water.

  • Gravel and riffle restoration

    Where the river was straightened and its gravel dug out, trout have nowhere to spawn. We hand-lay clean, graded gravel to rebuild the riffles. In 2025 we restored 212 m at Hollin Brook alone.

  • Volunteer planting days

    A Saturday rota from October to March. We plant sedge, water mint and hazel along bare banks, and every plant is logged to a map. Tools, gloves and a flask of tea are on us.

  • Invasive species removal

    Himalayan balsam crowds out native bank plants and leaves soil bare for winter floods. We pull it before it seeds, bag it for composting and mark each patch by GPS so crews can return.

  • Citizen-science monitoring

    Trained volunteers kick-sample for mayfly, stonefly and caddis larvae every month at 57 sites. Their counts are the river’s report card, and we publish the bad years as well as the good.

  • Hedge and bank planting

    Hazel, willow and alder bind the banks against erosion and shade the water in August, when trout are most at risk of heat stress. Our whips are grown from local stock at Marlpit Farm nursery.

  • School and corporate days

    Teachers and staff teams get a half-day on the river, with waders in sizes 4 to 14, a survey they can run in class and a site card to take home. We handle the risk assessment.

How your gift works

Three steps from your pound to the water.

  1. 1

    Your gift pays for a named job

    £15 buys 2 m² of spawning gravel. £40 funds a season of bank planting on one stretch. You choose the river, and the job list is public before you give.

  2. 2

    We do the work with people you can meet

    Field officers agree every job with the landowner, plan the volunteer rota and sign off the work against a survey before a single bucket of gravel goes in.

  3. 3

    You see what changed

    Within 60 days you receive a site card: before and after photographs, the monthly mayfly count and a line-by-line account of what your gift paid for.
