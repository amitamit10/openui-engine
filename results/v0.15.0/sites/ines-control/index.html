What I shoot

Six kinds of work, one way of looking.

Every commission starts from the same question: where is the light coming from, and how long will it stay there?

  • Weddings, in order

    A documentary day from the first coffee to the last dance, shot on film and digital so the pictures read as memory rather than staging.

  • Editorial portraits

    One window, one lens and a long conversation. Sittings run ninety minutes in a room you choose, lit by a single source and a reflector.

  • Restaurants and kitchens

    Plates, hands and steam in the quiet hour before service. Menu, chef and room photography, delivered in the sizes your site, print and social need.

  • Boutique hotels

    Rooms, courtyards and rooftops photographed at the hours guests will see them, with the blue hour and the noon light both planned in advance.

  • Film and digital

    Colour negative for skin and warmth, digital for low light. Every roll is scanned by hand, and you receive both sets of files.

  • Light scouting

    Before any paid shoot I visit the location at the hour we will shoot, track the sun and note where the shade falls. The plan goes into your brief.

About

Eleven years of Lisbon light, one camera at a time.

Ines moved to Lisbon in 2014 with two film bodies, a rented flat in Graça and a plan to photograph the city only in the light it already had. The work is still that: a studio of one, no backdrops, and a habit of waiting for the window to do most of the work.

How a shoot works

  1. 1

    Tell me the date and the light you want

    Send the date, the place and a few lines about the story. I reply within two working days with availability and a short proposal.

  2. 2

    We walk the location together

    For weddings and interiors I scout at the same hour as the shoot. We agree a shot list of around forty frames and a plan for weather.

  3. 3

    The shoot, then your gallery in 19 days

    I shoot quietly, then deliver a private gallery of edited digital files. Film scans arrive as files or as prints, whichever you prefer.
