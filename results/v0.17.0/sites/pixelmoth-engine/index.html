The game

Six small things we fussed over until they glowed.

  • Rooms drawn by hand

    All 63 rooms were inked on paper before they were coloured. You can still see the pen in the walls, the wobble in the windows and the one cat we kept.

  • Tiles that remember

    Slide, rotate and reconnect lantern cords and pipes. The board keeps every move, so you can always step one back without losing the thread.

  • A tide, not a timer

    The sea rises and falls on its own slow clock. There are no countdowns and no fail states. Come back when the water is low and the channels are open.

  • Lantern hints

    Stuck? A lantern drifts toward the next lamp you can light. Each hint dims the longer you hold it, so the final step still belongs to you.

  • A soundtrack that breathes

    Piano, music box and a far-off foghorn. The score thins out at low tide and swells when a lamp catches, so the island always sounds like it is listening.

  • A map that greens

    The island map fills with moss as each lamp returns. Two of the Collector's Edition pages are fold-out versions of it, so you can compare your progress by hand.

Demo

How a night on the island plays

  1. 1

    Light the first lamp

    At dusk the keeper's lamp flickers. Pick one room from the map. Each holds a single lamp, a few drifting boats and a question about the moss.

  2. 2

    Tend the room

    Turn the lenses, slide the lantern cord and let the water in. Every puzzle has a quiet solution, and most of them have two.

  3. 3

    Sail on

    When the lamp catches, the tide turns and a new channel opens. Save anywhere, close the lid, and the island will keep its place.

The demo is free on PC and Switch. Three islands, the Kettle Room and the first night's soundtrack.
