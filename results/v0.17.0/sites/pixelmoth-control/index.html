The game

Six things that make the island feel lived in

Every system is built around one idea: the island keeps changing when you are not looking, and it rewards you for noticing.

  • Rooms drawn by hand

    Every room starts as ink on paper. We kept the wobbly lines, so no two stairwells match and the lighthouse still shows the marks of our hands.

  • Tides that move the puzzle

    The sea rises and falls on a six-hour clock. Set a boat at low water and the bridge you built is gone by morning, so plan your night around the tide.

  • Ivy you untangle by hand

    Pull a vine and its roots answer back. A path opens, a nest drops from the rafters and a bell rings somewhere out on the water.

  • A lamp that keeps a log

    Your beam remembers every room you lit. Return to any night to see the path you took, then try a different route to find the one the keeper meant.

  • A score that thins with the water

    The Lantern Tapes soundtrack, 26 tracks by Ines Okafor-Lind, follows the tide: strings fall away at low water and the harp returns with the rising light.

  • A field journal, filled in

    Sketch the moths, moles and mossy beasts you meet. The journal holds 140 species, each with a note in our own handwriting and a line about where to find it.

Demo · 90 minutes, free

How a night on the island plays

The demo covers the first three rooms and the landing at dusk. Your progress carries straight into the full game.

  1. 1

    Wake the lamp

    Start on the landing at dusk. The lamp is dim, and the first switch sits where your eye falls. Early hints are gentle and never take the puzzle away from you.

  2. 2

    Read the room

    Each room is a puzzle built from tide, ivy and light. The pattern is always drawn somewhere in the room, in the wallpaper, a stain or a sketched bird, if you look closely.

  3. 3

    Carry the light

    Solve enough rooms and the beam swings out over the water to show the next night's path. Finish a night and you can wander back to any room whenever you like.

  • 96hand-drawn rooms
  • 340puzzles, from tide-timed to ivy-tangled
  • 42hours of play in a median first run
  • 11languages, with subtitles on every tide bell
